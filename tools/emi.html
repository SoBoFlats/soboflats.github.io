Common Questions
How is EMI calculated?›

EMI is calculated using the standard banking formula:

  • EMI = P × r × (1+r)n ÷ ((1+r)n − 1)
  • P = Loan amount (principal)
  • r = Monthly interest rate (annual rate ÷ 12 ÷ 100)
  • n = Total number of months (tenure × 12)
What other charges come with a home loan?›

Apart from EMI, banks charge several one-time and recurring fees:

  • Processing Fee — 0.25% to 1% of loan amount (one-time)
  • Legal & Technical Charges — ₹5,000–15,000 for property verification
  • Stamp Duty & Registration — On the loan agreement (varies by state)
  • MODT / CERSAI Charges — Mortgage registration fees
  • Insurance — Home loan protection plan (optional but recommended)
  • Prepayment/Foreclosure Fee — Nil for floating rate (RBI rule), up to 2% for fixed rate
What EMI can I afford?›

Banks use the FOIR (Fixed Obligations to Income Ratio) to decide eligibility:

  • Total EMIs (all loans) should be below 40–50% of monthly take-home salary
  • Example: ₹1,00,000 salary → max EMI around ₹40,000–50,000
  • Higher salary brackets may get slightly relaxed ratios
  • Existing car loan, credit card EMIs etc. reduce your eligible amount
How can I reduce my EMI?›
  • Larger down payment — reduces loan principal directly
  • Longer tenure — lowers EMI but increases total interest paid
  • Lower interest rate — negotiate with your bank, or consider balance transfer to another bank
  • Part-prepayments — even ₹1–2 lakh/year extra can save lakhs in interest over the loan life
  • Floating vs Fixed — floating rates are typically 0.5–1% lower than fixed
What tax benefits do I get on a home loan?›

Under the old tax regime:

  • Section 80C — Up to ₹1.5 lakh deduction on principal repayment
  • Section 24(b) — Up to ₹2 lakh deduction on interest paid (self-occupied)
  • Section 80EEA — Additional ₹1.5 lakh for first-time buyers (stamp value up to ₹45 lakh)
  • Joint loan — Both co-borrowers can claim deductions separately

Note: New tax regime offers lower rates but fewer deductions. Consult your CA.

Fixed vs Floating rate — which is better?›
  • Floating rate — Changes with RBI repo rate. Currently cheaper (8–9%). No prepayment penalty. Best for most borrowers.
  • Fixed rate — Stays same for the entire tenure (or initial years). Usually 0.5–1.5% higher. Good if you expect rates to rise significantly.
  • Hybrid — Fixed for first 2–3 years, then switches to floating. Offered by some banks.
  • Most Indian home loans today are on floating rate linked to EBLR/RLLR
Can I prepay or foreclose my loan early?›
  • Floating rate loans — Zero prepayment/foreclosure charges (RBI mandate since 2012)
  • Fixed rate loans — Banks may charge up to 2–4% of outstanding principal
  • Best strategy — Make partial prepayments annually; even small amounts in early years save significantly since interest component is highest then
  • You can choose to reduce tenure (keeps EMI same, saves more interest) or reduce EMI (keeps tenure same, gives monthly relief)
