Common Questions
What is RERA Carpet Area?›

As per RERA Section 2(k), carpet area is defined as:

  • The net usable floor area of an apartment
  • Includes: area covered by internal partition walls
  • Excludes: external walls, service shafts, exclusive balcony/verandah, exclusive open terrace

This is the legal standard all builders must quote. RERA is a central act — the definition under Section 2(k) is the same across all states (Maharashtra, UP, Karnataka, Tamil Nadu, etc.).

Key point: RERA Carpet includes the thickness of your internal walls (bedroom walls, bathroom walls, kitchen partition) — so it is 3–7% more than the actual space you walk on.

What is Net Usable Carpet Area?›
  • Net Usable Carpet = the floor area where you can actually walk, place furniture, or lay a carpet
  • It is RERA Carpet minus internal partition walls
  • Typically 3–7% less than RERA Carpet (5% is standard in Mumbai)

Example: If RERA Carpet = 1,000 sq ft, and internal walls occupy ~50 sq ft (5%), then Net Usable = 950 sq ft. That 950 sq ft is what you actually live in.

Why it matters: When comparing two projects, checking usable carpet area gives a true apples-to-apples comparison. A builder with thicker walls or more partitions will have a larger gap between RERA carpet and usable carpet.

RERA Carpet vs old “Carpet Area” — how do they differ?›

Before RERA (pre-2017), there was no standard definition of carpet area. Builders often inflated it:

  • Some included dry balconies, flower beds, enclosed verandahs
  • Some included half the external wall thickness
  • Result: old “carpet area” was 5–15% higher than today’s RERA carpet for the same flat

Post-RERA: The definition is strict and uniform. Only internal walls are included; everything else (balconies, verandahs, terraces, external walls, service shafts) is excluded.

Resale tip: If you’re comparing an old brochure (pre-RERA) with a new RERA certificate, the old number will look bigger. Don’t be misled — ask for the RERA-registered carpet area.

What is “loading” and why does it vary so much?›
  • Loading = the percentage by which super built-up exceeds carpet area
  • It covers external walls, balconies, and your share of all common areas

Why it varies:

  • SoBo premium towers (30–35%): Grand double-height lobbies, concierge, multiple lifts, sky lounge
  • Suburban buildings (25–30%): Standard lobby, 2 lifts, basic amenities
  • Ultra luxury (35–45%): Private lifts, podium parking, club house, infinity pool, landscaped terraces
  • Affordable housing (18–22%): Minimal common areas, staircase-only, basic design
  • Commercial (35–40%): Large lobbies, fire corridors, service floors

Buyer tip: Lower loading = more carpet per rupee. But also check what amenities the loading pays for — a slightly higher loading with a great club house may be worth it.

How to calculate effective price per carpet sq ft?›

Most builders quote rate per super built-up sq ft. The true cost of your usable space is much higher:

  • Effective RERA carpet rate = (SBU rate × SBU area) ÷ RERA Carpet Area
  • Effective usable rate = (SBU rate × SBU area) ÷ Net Usable Carpet Area

Example: ₹25,000/SBU sq ft × 1,500 SBU = ₹3.75 Cr total

  • At 35% loading: RERA carpet = ~1,111 sq ft → ₹33,750/carpet sq ft
  • At 5% internal walls: Usable = ~1,056 sq ft → ₹35,511/usable sq ft

That’s 42% more than the quoted rate! Use the Price Impact section above to calculate this instantly.

Pro tip: When comparing two properties, calculate effective usable rate for both. The one with lower usable rate gives you more space per rupee.

Is RERA Carpet Area the same across all states?›
  • Yes. RERA 2016 is a Central Act — Section 2(k) defines carpet area identically for all states
  • State bodies (Maharashtra RERA, UP-RERA, Karnataka RERA, Tamil Nadu RERA, etc.) all follow the same definition
  • The only variation is in enforcement strictness, not in the definition itself
  • This tool uses the uniform RERA definition for all calculations
What are dry balcony and flower bed? Are they in RERA Carpet?›
  • Dry balcony: Small utility balcony (usually near kitchen) for washing machine, drying clothes. Typically 20–40 sq ft.
  • Flower bed: Narrow open ledge outside windows, 5–15 sq ft. Not walkable, but builders used to count it.
  • RERA excludes both from carpet area — they fall under “exclusive balcony/verandah”
  • They are included in Built-up area
  • Old brochures often included dry balcony in carpet area, inflating it by 3–5%
  • Enclosed balconies (with glass/grill) are also excluded from RERA carpet, even though you can use the space. They get FSI concessions in building approvals.
What documents should I check for carpet area?›
  • RERA registration page: Every registered project lists RERA carpet area for each unit type. Check your state’s RERA website.
  • Agreement for Sale: Must mention RERA carpet area since 2017. Compare this with what the salesperson quoted.
  • Sanctioned plan: The architect’s drawing approved by the municipal corporation. Shows exact room dimensions.
  • Carpet Area Statement: Some builders provide a detailed statement showing how RERA carpet is calculated (room-by-room).
  • OC / CC: Occupancy Certificate mentions total built-up. Cross-check with your agreement.

Red flag: If a builder refuses to share the RERA carpet area or says “we only quote super built-up,” it may indicate unfavourable loading.
