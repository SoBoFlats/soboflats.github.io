Common Questions
How is property ROI calculated?›

Property ROI combines two components:

  • Capital Appreciation — (Current Value − Purchase Price) as % of purchase price
  • Net Rental Yield — (Annual Rent − Vacancy − Expenses) ÷ Purchase Price × 100
  • Total ROI = Capital Gains + Total Net Rental over holding period, as % of total investment (purchase + ~7% costs + interiors if any)
  • CAGR (Compound Annual Growth Rate) annualizes the total return for easy comparison
  • Vacancy & increment adjustments make the calculation more realistic than flat annual projections
What is a good ROI for real estate in India?›
  • 8–12% annually is considered good, combining rental yield + appreciation
  • Rental yield in Indian metros: 2–4% for residential, 6–9% for commercial
  • Capital appreciation in prime areas: 5–10% annually over 5+ year periods
  • Mumbai South (SoBo) typically sees 4–7% appreciation with 2–3% rental yield
  • Compare against alternatives: FD (7%), mutual funds (12–15%), gold (8–10%)
What expenses should I include?›
  • Society maintenance — Monthly charges (₹5–25/sq ft in Mumbai)
  • Property tax — Annual municipal tax (varies by city)
  • Insurance — Home insurance premium (~0.1% of property value)
  • Repairs & upkeep — Budget 0.5–1% of property value annually
  • Brokerage — 1–2 months rent when finding new tenants (average over years)
  • Interior depreciation — If furnished, factor in wear and replacement costs
How does vacancy loss affect returns?›
  • 1 month vacancy reduces annual rental income by ~8.3%
  • Premium properties in SoBo typically see 1–2 months vacancy between tenants
  • Furnished apartments may have shorter vacancy if priced right
  • Commercial property can have longer vacancies (3–6 months) but higher rents compensate
  • Tip: Factor in 1 month minimum even for good locations — tenant transitions take time
What is a standard rent increment in India?›
  • 5% annually is the most common increment in Leave & License agreements
  • Range: 5–8% is typical, with 10%+ for prime commercial spaces
  • 11-month L&L agreements in Maharashtra typically specify 5% escalation on renewal
  • Lock-in period: First year is usually fixed; increment starts from year 2
  • Market conditions affect actual increments — high demand areas may command higher increases
How does leverage (home loan) affect ROI?›
  • Leverage amplifies returns — If you pay 20% down (₹10L on a ₹50L property), a 10% appreciation gives you 50% return on YOUR cash
  • Interest cost eats into returns — EMI interest on an 80% loan at 8.5% is significant
  • Tax benefit on home loan interest (Sec 24b: up to ₹2L) and principal (Sec 80C: up to ₹1.5L) partially offsets
  • Break-even: Property must appreciate + yield above your loan interest rate for positive leveraged returns
  • Risk: Leverage works both ways — if property value drops, losses are magnified
Should I include interior cost in my ROI calculation?›
  • Yes — interiors are part of your total investment and affect true ROI
  • Typical costs: ₹500–1000/sq ft (basic), ₹1000–2000/sq ft (premium), ₹2000+/sq ft (luxury)
  • Furnished properties command 15–30% higher rent, partially offsetting the investment
  • Depreciation: Interiors lose value over 5–8 years and may need refresh
  • Resale impact: Good interiors can increase sale price, but buyer taste varies
Real estate vs mutual funds — which is better?›
  • Mutual funds — Higher liquidity, lower entry barrier, 12–15% CAGR (Nifty 50), no maintenance. But: no rental income, no leverage, more volatile
  • Real estate — Tangible asset, rental cash flow, leverage via home loan, tax benefits. But: illiquid, high entry cost, maintenance, tenant risk
  • Best approach: Diversify — use the comparison above to see how your property stacks up
  • Emotional value: Owning a home provides security that financial instruments don’t
